## Defensive-loop finding and fix

The first v3 draft allowed unlimited survival Retreat after the ordinary two-action caution budget when the actor remained critical/high-fear/attack-disabled. This was believable locally but reintroduced encounter loops.

Using `tools/measure_m027_profiles.gd`, 100 paired seeds per matchup initially produced stalls:

- Feral Dog / Wolf: 16 stalls
- Raider / Giant Beetle: 48 stalls
- Raider / Armored Raider: 13 stalls

The policy was changed to allow exactly one emergency survival Retreat after the two ordinary cautious actions. After that, no further defensive action is generated until an Attack resets caution.

After the later conservative-retreat and armor-coverage refinement, the same 100 paired seeds (200 encounters per matchup) were rerun:

| Matchup | A wins | B wins | Stalls | Simulation errors |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Feral Dog / Wolf | 14 | 186 | 0 | 0 |
| Raider / Giant Beetle | 50 | 150 | 0 | 0 |
| Raider / Armored Raider | 4 | 196 | 0 | 0 |

These are policy-change evidence, not final balance targets. They differ materially from M027's simple basic-melee results, so Threat Rating recalibration should occur after M028 integration rather than before.

## Information boundary

The policy does not score exact target armor value, target ability scores, target attack damage, RNG state, scheduler ready time, expected damage, hit probability or future actions.

Armor selection uses only a dominant visible profile ID plus a coarse hit-weight coverage band: none, partial (<35%), or substantial (>=35%). Exact armor magnitude remains hidden. Partial coverage weakens profile matchup signals and does not grant penetration-option bonus; substantial coverage enables the full qualitative matchup and penetration factors. Penetration value itself is read only from the actor's own action data.

## Qualitative headless review

A follow-up behavior review inspected candidate scores and full action sequences rather than only assertions.

What looked good:

- Longsword uses basic Cut against an unarmored target and Thrust against MAIL.
- Knife basic and Quick Stab tie on a healthy unarmored target, so deterministic candidate order keeps basic; Quick Stab becomes preferred when the target is critical.
- Warhammer basic wins against an unarmored target while Crushing Blow becomes preferred against meaningful armor.
- Aggressive healthy Maul users accept Overhead Smash; critical/high-fear users strongly prefer survival movement.
- 2+1 defensive allowance prevents stalls in the paired batch and eventually forces encounter progress.

The armor-coverage issue found in the first qualitative review was fixed with coarse hit-weight bands. On the human body, Iron Helmet is 15% and therefore partial; Scrap Plate torso is 45% and Chain Shirt torso+arms is 65%, both substantial. Direct review now gives:

- Warhammer vs unarmored: Basic
- Warhammer vs helmet-only RIGID: Basic
- Warhammer vs torso RIGID: Crushing Blow
- Warhammer vs Chain Shirt MAIL: Crushing Blow
- Longsword vs helmet-only RIGID: Basic
- Longsword vs torso RIGID: Thrust
- Longsword vs Chain Shirt MAIL: Thrust

Retreat was also made more conservative. It is no longer generated merely because a legal step opens distance. Except for lost attack capability, it requires `fear + injury - aggression/2 >= 60`. The distance/exposure utility bonuses were also reduced. In the 40-seed qualitative action sample, Raider vs Giant Beetle `survive` goals dropped from 446 to 202 while all 40 encounters still terminated. Armored Raider vs Raider dropped from 172 to 117 survives. A representative trace now keeps fighting through ordinary wounded state and begins retreat behavior only at severe/critical injury.

The remaining low-HP 2+1 retreat/approach pattern is deliberately bounded and no longer common enough to block integration. Revisit once real escape goals, world exits, allies or terrain objectives give Retreat a destination beyond buying distance.
